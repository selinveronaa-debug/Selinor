Why a house works

Around 500 BC the poet Simonides could name every guest at a collapsed banquet hall by remembering where each one sat. Your brain stores places almost effortlessly, so we put facts in places.

  1. 1
    Familiar, distinct, in order. Each room is a stop, each object a spot, always walked the same way.
  2. 2
    Weird works. A fact that moves, smashes or sings on the sofa sticks. A quiet one fades.
  3. 3
    Recall, then space it. Test yourself on the route, then walk it again after 1, 3 and 7 days.
26 → 62

words recalled out of 72 after 40 days of 30-minute memory-palace practice. Most of the gain was still there four months later.

Dresler et al., Neuron, 2017 · summary

THE PROBLEM

Students study hard, the wrong way.

Most students reread their notes, which feels productive but fades fast. Memory research has known for decades what works: test yourself, space it out, and give facts a place. RoomRecall builds all three into one app, for any course, in any language.

84%of students name rereading as a study strategy, one of the weakest ways to learn
61% vs 40%remembered after a week when students tested themselves instead of rereading

WHY IT LOOKS LIKE THIS

Students already chose the vibe. Their study tools never did.

Millions of students study to lofi videos: a cosy room at night, rain on the window, fairy lights, a sleepy cat. The tools that actually make you remember look like grey flashcards and PDFs. RoomRecall puts the method that works inside the room students already want to sit in.
